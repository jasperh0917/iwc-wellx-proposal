# Why this deal is structured the way it is

CEO rationale memo for the IWC × WellX commercial deal.

Last updated: 2026-05-12. Reflects the revised pricing structure (membership fee absorbs the 15K floor + integrations).

---

## The deal in one sentence

**$300K guaranteed annual revenue, $1.45M at 100K members, and IWC has every reason to push the upper number — because they've already paid for the lower one.**

---

## What changed from the original structure

Earlier draft separated the floor ($25K MF + $20 × 15K members = $325K) into three line items: a small membership fee, a per-member rate, and a contractual floor. Buyers found it confusing — three lines that all priced the same baseline differently. We restructured to **one line item**: $300K annual membership fee that includes the first 15,000 activated members plus all platform integrations. Marginal rates only apply above 15K.

Revenue at every member count is mathematically identical to the old structure. The change is presentational, not financial. But the new structure has two operational consequences:

1. **The 2-year discount shrunk from ~$75K → $25K.** Old structure waived MF on 2-year + waived WL. New structure waives only WL. The 2-year hook is weaker — see "where the term-length lever went" below.
2. **The activation flywheel argument got stronger.** When the floor is explicit and the buyer literally writes a $300K check for 15K seats, every unactivated member becomes a much more visible "wasted spend" line on their P&L. Stronger incentive to activate aggressively.

---

## The three levers, what each one does

### 1. $300K annual membership fee — the take-or-pay

Includes:
- 15,000 activated members
- App + admin dashboard access
- Tiered pricing model unlocks for growth above 15K
- Integration with telemedicine + rewards systems
- (Implicit) integration work, content calibration, training during onboarding

This is the revenue the deal underwrites. Paid annually for the contract term. Not waivable. If IWC activates 8,000 members, they still pay $300K — that's the contractual floor in the new structure. If they activate 14,000, they still pay $300K. Activation 15,001 is the first "additional cost" they ever see.

### 2. 5-band marginal pricing above 15,000

| Band | Members | $/member/yr | PHP/member/yr |
|------|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Band 1 | 15,001 – 30,000 | $18 | ₱1,080 |
| Band 2 | 30,001 – 45,000 | $16 | ₱960 |
| Band 3 | 45,001 – 60,000 | $14 | ₱840 |
| Band 4 | 60,001 – 75,000 | $12 | ₱720 |
| Band 5 | 75,000+ | $10 | ₱600 |

Step-down of $2 per band. Same "growth pays for itself" curve as before, just relabeled now that the first 15K are pre-paid. Effective $/member at scale:

| Members | Annual revenue | Effective $/member |
|---------|---------------|--------------------|
| 15,000 | $300K | $20.00 |
| 30,000 | $570K | $19.00 |
| 50,000 | $880K | $17.60 |
| 75,000 | $1.20M | $16.00 |
| 100,000 | $1.45M | $14.50 |
| 150,000 | $1.95M | $13.00 |

### 3. $25K white-label — the 2-year sweetener

Same as before. One-time $25K, waived on 2-year. The waivable amount is now smaller than in the original structure, so its weight as a term-length lever is reduced. This is the only fee in the deal that gets discounted for term commitment.

## The activation flywheel — why the floor creates aligned incentives

This is the part the CEO needs to internalize.

**Once IWC pays the $300K membership fee, every unactivated member is wasted spend on their P&L.**

The $300K is sized to cover 15,000 activated members. That's roughly $20/member. If IWC only activates 8,000 members, they're effectively paying $37.50/member for 8,000 instead of $20/member for 15,000. The math is unforgiving and visible to their finance team every quarter.

So:

- IWC's HR teams will push enrollment — they bought it
- IWC marketing will promote the app to existing members — they bought it
- IWC member services will surface app downloads in every interaction — they bought it
- We don't run activation campaigns. They do.

This is the bet: **once IWC has paid for 15K activated members, they will activate at least 30K-50K because their internal economics force them to.** A pre-paid asset that's not being used is a problem they have to solve — and the only way to solve it is to activate more members.

Compared to the old structure (where the floor was split across three line items), the new $300K-as-a-single-line-item makes the "wasted spend" visceral. Their CFO will know exactly what 15,000 seats cost.

## Why IWC should pay for it — the customer's ROI story

The $300K is justifiable to IWC because we deliver concrete, hard-currency savings. Floor revenue to IWC = $300K (~₱18M/yr). They need to unlock at least ₱18M of value to break even.

**Operational savings IWC unlocks** (numbers from slide 11, scaled to 15K members):

| Savings lever | Per-member impact | At 15K activated |
|---|---|---|
| Call center deflection (4–6 inquiries × ₱30–60) | ₱120–360/yr | ₱1.8M–5.4M/yr |
| Telemedicine vs. clinic substitution (1/member/yr) | ₱500–1,500 | ₱7.5M–22.5M/yr |
| Clinic redirection to IWC owned/partner (4 visits/member) | ₱200–800/visit | ₱1.5M–9.6M/yr |
| Retention uplift (1 renewal per 100 members) | ₱20K–50K | ₱3M+/yr |

**Just call-center deflection + telemedicine substitution at the LOW end (₱1.8M + ₱7.5M = ₱9.3M) is more than half the ₱18M floor.** With clinic redirection and retention factored in at the low end, IWC clears the floor inside 4-6 months. Everything else — claims reduction, AI co-build value, brand differentiation, member NPS — is pure margin.

The $300K is engineered to pay back fast on operational savings alone. The CEO can defend it as a cost-reduction investment, not a discretionary marketing spend.

## Where the term-length lever went

We need to talk about this honestly. The original structure had ~$75K of fees waived on 2-year — a tangible "you save real money" argument for IWC's CFO. The new structure waives only $25K (White Label). The 2-year hook is meaningfully weaker.

**Three options to compensate (pick one before negotiation):**

**A) Price-lock guarantee on 2-year.** Lock the $300K membership fee + marginal rates for both years. On 1-year, reserve the right to reprice in Year 2. Costs us nothing if we wouldn't have repriced anyway; gives IWC's CFO a defensible reason to take the term.

**B) Year-2 rate step-down on 2-year.** $300K Y1, $280K Y2 — saves IWC $20K but only if they commit to the second year. Easier to explain than (A) but costs us $20K in revenue.

**C) Activation milestone refund.** If IWC activates 25K+ in Year 1, refund $50K of the Y2 MF. Aligns our money with their activation execution — we only pay out if they hit a number that grew our marginal-band revenue by ~$200K. Net positive for us.

I'd lean toward **(C)**. It costs us up to $50K but only when we're also earning ~$200K more in marginal revenue from the activation push. The refund is in IWC's control, which makes it credible as a deal sweetener.

---

## What we sacrifice vs. what we get

| Sacrifice | Gain |
|---|---|
| $25K white-label fee (waived on 2-year) | 2-year contract term |
| Up to $50K in milestone refunds (if option C above) | Aligned activation incentives + $200K+ in marginal revenue if they hit |
| Marginal rate compresses from $18 → $10 across bands | A scaling story IWC can sell internally to their board |
| Some negotiating room on the membership fee | Cleanest possible 1-line pricing pitch |

Net at 2-year floor: $600K guaranteed minimum, $1.78M at 50K activations, $2.40M at 75K, $2.90M at 100K. Even after waivers + potential milestone refunds, we're underwriting a high-floor, high-ceiling deal.

## How to use the levers in negotiation

If IWC pushes on…

- **Membership fee size** → *"It's the price of including 15,000 members plus all your integration and platform work for a year. Without it we'd quote per-member from member 1 and a separate setup fee. This structure is simpler and you know what 2 years costs you up front."*
- **Term length** → *"We waive White Label on 2-year. If price-lock or a Year-2 step-down matters, we have room to structure that — but we need the commitment to underwrite the integration build."*
- **Per-member rate** → *"$18 is Band 1, only above the 15K covered by the membership. Rate compresses fast as you grow — at 100K members you're paying $14.50 effective. Most providers hold the rate flat."*
- **Annual recurrence of the membership fee** → *"It's annual because the value is annual — platform access, dashboard, integrations, support, ongoing content calibration. One-time fees don't fund those obligations."*

If IWC tries to negotiate the membership fee down:
- *"The fee bundles 15,000 seats + integration value. If we strip out the 15,000 seats, we charge $20/member from member 1 plus a separate setup fee ($25K+). The bundled price is cheaper for you in the floor case."*
- *"Our floor is sized so a credible activation effort on your side recovers it within 4-6 months on call deflection and telemedicine substitution alone. If you can't put 15K members on the platform, the conversation we should have is whether this is the right deal shape."*

## The strategic bet, stated plainly

**Belief:** Once IWC has paid the $300K, they will activate well above 15K — because their own P&L forces them to.

**If we're right:** $1M+ ARR within four quarters, a flagship reference customer at scale, AI co-build compounds into Year 2.

**If we're wrong:** $300K guaranteed for two years, plus the integration IP stays useful for the next deal.

The downside is bounded. The upside is asymmetric. Same bet as before — just told more cleanly now that the floor is one line on the invoice.
